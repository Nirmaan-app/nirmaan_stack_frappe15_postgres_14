Concurrency & Locking in BoQ Upload

What happens when two people work on the same BoQ at the same time — and the design that stops them silently destroying each other's work. This is a design explainer: it explains the problem, the two things we already have, and the proposed solution with diagrams. No code yet.

design locked · 20 decisions facts verified build not started

1 · The one-sentence problem

The whole app is multi-user, but almost nothing in the BoQ wizard stops two people editing the same thing at once. Most of the time that's harmless — but in a handful of places it silently corrupts data or throws away a whole work session. Here are the three that actually hurt:

🧹

Config gets wiped

Saving a sheet's parser config replaces the whole blob. If two people configure the same sheet, the second save silently erases the first person's entire column-mapping session.

phase: Spoke / Config · set_sheet_config
🌳

Review tree corrupts

A "restructure" rewrites parent pointers across many rows at once, checked against a stale snapshot. Two at once can create a cycle or orphan that neither request's guard catches.

phase: Review · save_review_restructure
👯

Two "current" versions

Committing computes max(version)+1 with no lock. Two commits of the same sheet both write is_current=1 → two current rows, which quietly breaks every downstream pricing read.

phase: Commit · commit_boq
🎯
The goal

Prevent the destructive collisions with a real, server-enforced, realtime single-editor lock — while keeping the recoverable ones friction-free with a simple "someone else is here" alert. And fix the commit race regardless, because it corrupts data silently.

2 · We already have two tools — and they're opposites

The starting point was two existing mechanisms. The key realization is that they sit at opposite ends of a spectrum and solve different problems. Understanding why is the whole design.

In-Progress PR

The "someone is editing this PR" yellow banner. It's Frappe-core doc_viewers presence — genuinely realtime, zero backend code… and zero enforcement. Two people can still both save. Blocking was even coded once and deliberately commented out.

realtime no teeth

BoQ Pricing

A real server-enforced single-editor lock — it actually rejects the second writer. But it has no realtime layer: the other user only finds out on page-load or when their save is rejected. It works; it just surfaces late.

real lock not realtime

💡
Two distinctions that unlock the whole design

Presence ≠ Lock. Knowing who is in the room is not the same as deciding who holds the pen.  ·  Server-authoritative ≠ Realtime-notified. A lock can perfectly protect the data (server rejects the write) while still being slow to tell anyone about it.

Concept

Presence, alerts, and locks are a spectrum

From "just tell me who's here" to "physically stop the second writer." Every BoQ phase gets placed somewhere on this line.

Presence"Who else has this open?"doc_viewers
Soft alertWarns, never blocks. Last-write-wins.PR banner
Hard lockServer rejects the 2nd writer.pricing lock
Realtime lockRejects and tells everyone live.the goal →

The In-Progress PR banner sits at Soft alert. The BoQ pricing lock sits at Hard lock. Neither is at the far right yet. The design moves the destructive BoQ phases to Realtime lock, and keeps the recoverable ones at Soft alert.

4 · Why the pricing lock feels "not realtime"

The pricing lock is the opposite: it genuinely rejects the second writer on the server. But it never pushes that fact to anyone. So a second user can invest a whole edit before finding out — the exact symptom that motivated this work.

Neither one is complete — so we fuse them

useEditingLock has the right lifecycle (heartbeat, sendBeacon cleanup, socket events) but doesn't actually enforce. The pricing lock has the right enforcement but no realtime or cleanup. The design takes the enforcement core from one and the realtime+cleanup layer from the other.

The solution

One fused lock, in three layers

Enforcement that can't be bypassed, a realtime layer so everyone sees state live, and a cleanup layer so locks free the instant you leave.

6 · The three layers

Enforcementreuse pricing lock
A deterministic sha1 primary key → the second concurrent insert collides → exactly one winner. Every write endpoint calls it and frappe.throws the loser. The reject writes nothing. This is the durable, unbypassable boundary.
Realtimenew · model on commission lock
A broadcast boq:lock_changed event (acquired / released / taken_over). Every screen listens, filters to its own sheet, ignores its own events → a second user flips to read-only within one socket round-trip. Self-heals on reconnect via a re-fetch.
Cleanupnew · model on useEditingLock
Release-on-leave (sendBeacon) frees the lock the instant you close the tab. A heartbeat keeps it alive while you're actively editing. Short ~2-min TTL means even a browser crash frees it fast — no 5-minute ghost.
♻️
Almost all the plumbing already exists

The realtime infra (screen-scoped socket listeners + broadcast events) is proven three times over in BoQ already (boq:parse_run_done, the AI passes). The lock engine exists in pricing. The heartbeat/sendBeacon pattern exists in the PR lock. This is mostly wiring known parts together, not new invention.

8 · Lifecycle — acquire, heartbeat, release, take over

A lock lives only as long as someone is actually editing. Leaving frees it immediately; a crash frees it within the short TTL; an admin can override a stuck one.

📝
The data-loss contract (when you lose a lock mid-edit)

Your unsaved draft is kept visible and clearly flagged "not saved" — never silently discarded, and never clobbered by a forced reload. We don't auto-merge it into the new editor's session (too risky). Plus a browser "you have unsaved changes" guard on navigate-away. Honest limit: we protect visibility & recovery, not automatic preservation.

9 · Presence comes back — as awareness, where it belongs

The PR doc_viewers mechanism isn't thrown away — it returns as the complementary awareness layer on top of the lock. One editor holds the pen; everyone else can watch read-only and see who's here.

🔒
The lock owns correctness

One server-enforced editor per sheet. This is what actually prevents lost work.

👥
doc_viewers owns awareness

A free "also here: B, C" presence list. They coexist instead of competing — this is the clean answer to "was my PR approach correct?" Yes, for awareness.

🚫
Not in scope: live cursors

Google-Docs-style cell-by-cell cursors are deferred — far beyond the correctness goal.

Applying it

Where each phase lands

Not every phase needs a hard lock. Destructive & unrecoverable → lock. Recoverable → soft alert. And the commit race needs its own fix entirely.

10 · Two locks, by tier

Config and Review both edit the same sheet's draft and are coupled (a re-parse from Config destroys Review's rows) — so they share one draft-tier lock. Pricing is a separate committed-tier lock. They never overlap in time.

Draft-tier lock

sha1(boq · sheet · "draft")
  • Spans Config + Review together
  • One "draft editor" per sheet
  • Reuses the pricing-lock doctype with a version = 0 sentinel — no new table, no migration
  • Reject marker BOQ_DRAFT_LOCKED

Committed-tier lock

sha1(boq · sheet · version)
  • The existing pricing lock, unchanged identity
  • One editor per committed sheet version
  • Gets retrofitted with the realtime + cleanup layers first (safe proving ground)
  • Reject marker BOQ_PRICING_LOCKED

12 · The commit race — verified & catastrophic

This one isn't an editor conflict — it's a classic read-then-write race with no guard at all. Two skeptical reviewers independently confirmed it against the live code (and checked live data: zero duplicates today, but nothing prevents it).

The fix — defense in depth:

1
App-level serialization

A commit_in_progress flag (mirroring the existing parse_in_progress) so the second commit waits and computes N+2 — clean UX, clear "a commit is already running."

2
Partial unique index (the real backstop)

UNIQUE (boq, sheet_name) WHERE is_current=1 on the BoQ Sheet + Committed Grid tiers. The database physically refuses a second current row — even across two app workers or a check-then-set gap.

3
Heal-then-constrain migration

Reconcile any existing duplicates first (keep the highest version as current, demote the rest — never delete frozen rows, and pick the same winning version across tiers), then add the index. Live data is clean today, so this is precautionary.

The third dimension

A one-way street: don't disturb downstream work

The locks so far stop two people fighting over the same screen. But the BoQ phases run in one direction — Config → Review → Commit → Pricing — and nothing stops someone in an earlier stage reaching back and destroying the work of someone in a later stage. That's a different problem, and it needs a third kind of guard.

13 · Three lock dimensions, not one

Everything up to here was same-stage — two peers on the same thing at the same time. The directional guard is orthogonal: it protects across the stage ladder, and the existing locks are blind to it.

Lock dimensionAnswers the question…Scope
Draft-tier lock D8"Is a peer editing Config/Review of this sheet right now?"(boq, sheet)

🔗
Why the pricing lock can't cover this

The pricing lock is version-scoped — it silently drops the instant a new commit_version is minted. That's exactly the rug-pull below: the act of re-committing destroys the very lock that would have protected it. So the directional guard must live above the version axis.

🧭
Ownership + the orphan contract

Author-or-Admin (D16): the person who did the work can always re-iterate it; only an Admin can authorize disturbing someone else's — the first role check in the otherwise role-less wizard. Orphan-and-surface (D17): nothing is deleted (every version is preserved); the displaced pricing is surfaced both ways — the actor sees what they'll strand, the returning author sees where it went — and copy-forward stays opt-in. Never auto-migrated (a partial migration that looks complete is a trap) and never auto-deleted.

16 · Five surgical guards

The existing "Finalized freeze" already blocks most backward writes, so the real gap is just five specific holes. Each gets one existence-check guard — extending a pattern already in the code, not a new lock subsystem.

🧱
A third axis, composed of parts we already have

The five guards are the state floor (a cheap existence check, the same throw-pattern as today's finalized freeze). The D8 locks supply the presence upgrade (naming a live victim → hard block). Attribution supplies ownership (Author-or-Admin). Two invariants are preserved: re-committing a sheet with no downstream pricing stays free forward progress, and the parse-vs-commit eligibility asymmetry is untouched.

Rollout & scope

How it ships — and what's deferred

Fix the dangerous bug first, prove the fused lock on the surface that already blocks, then spread to the new phases.

🛡️
Why this order

The commit fix is independent and it's the only silent corruption → it ships first. Retrofitting the pricing lock (which already blocks) proves the new realtime+cleanup machinery on low-risk ground, before we debut brand-new blocking on Config/Review. The directional guard's state-floor half (pure existence checks) has almost no dependencies and can ride alongside Phase A to kill the silent rug-pull early; its presence-upgrade reuses Phase B's locks, so the full tiered guard lands in Phase C. A server-side break-glass flag (default on) can disable all enforcement instantly if anything misbehaves in production.

19 · One open decision

⚠️
Hub general-specs (D11) — needs your call

Fixing the general-specs "replace-all clobber" turned out not to be backend-only (the UI submits the whole set). Two options: (a) rewrite it into an additive per-sheet toggle (eliminates the race, more work FE+BE), or (b) lean: keep the one-Save endpoint and add an optimistic compare-and-set — if the set changed since you loaded it, reject with "specs changed, reload." Smaller, preserves the current UX, turns silent clobber into a visible, recoverable conflict.
